A fly's nervous system, as a cloud

The animal

This is the complete central nervous system of one adult male fruit fly (Drosophila melanogaster). The rounded upper part is the brain, which sits in the head. The long tail is the ventral nerve cord, the fly's equivalent of a spinal cord, which runs through the thorax and abdomen and drives the legs, wings and body. A narrow neck connective joins them. The whole thing is about a millimetre long.

What a connectome is

A connectome is a complete wiring diagram: every neuron and every connection between them. To make this one, the tissue was shaved away 8 nanometres at a time (a human hair is about 10,000 times thicker) and each face was photographed with an electron microscope. AI from Google Research then traced every neuron through the stack of images, and people spent roughly 44 person-years checking and correcting the result.

Neuropils and cell bodies

Insect neurons are arranged inside-out compared with ours. The cell bodies (the part with the nucleus) sit in a thin rind on the outside. The inside is neuropil: a dense felt of axons and dendrites where all the actual connections are made. Anatomists divide this neuropil into named compartments, and each compartment tends to do a recognisable job.

Each coloured cloud here is one such compartment. The points are random samples of its volume, not individual neurons, so a bigger, denser cloud means a bigger compartment. Switch on Cell bodies in the bar below to add the true position of every traced neuron's cell body; you will see them wrap the outside like a shell.

How to read the colours

Compartments that belong to the same system share a hue family. Left and right copies of a paired region share one colour.

Getting oriented

Anterior looks at the fly face-on: the two big blue lobes are the optic lobes behind each compound eye, and the nerve cord is foreshortened behind. Dorsal looks down from above. Lateral shows the full length from the side. Hover any region in the list on the left to isolate it and read what it does.
